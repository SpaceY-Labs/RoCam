\title{Multi-Stage Training Evolution for Small Rocket Detection:\\
From YOLO26s to YOLO26s-P2 with Robustness-Enhanced Pipeline}
\author{\authname}
\date{March 2026}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  We present the complete training evolution of a small-rocket detection
  system for the \progname{} project, spanning three major pipeline
  iterations (V1, V2, V3).  Starting from a vanilla \textsc{Yolo26s}
  baseline ($\text{mAP}_{50\text{-}95} = 0.694$), we upgrade to the
  \textsc{Yolo26s-P2} architecture with a stride-4 detection head and
  introduce COCO hard negative mining.  Although the initial
  architectural change degrades performance to $0.614$, a three-stage
  fine-tuning protocol (V1) recovers it to $0.750$.  In V2, we
  identify optimizer mismatch and extend training to reach $0.762$.
  However, V2's fine-tuning stages consistently regress due to a
  \emph{training regime shock}---the transition from 4-GPU DDP to
  single-GPU with different batch size, rectangular inference, and
  non-functional custom augmentations.  V3 resolves all three root
  causes: (1)~single-GPU training with gradient accumulation
  (\texttt{nbs=128}) maintains regime consistency across phases,
  (2)~the native \texttt{augmentations} parameter replaces the failed
  monkey-patch approach, and (3)~aggressive small-target augmentation
  (\texttt{scale=0.25}, \texttt{erasing=0.30}, 9~Albumentations
  transforms) is applied for the first time.  The final V3 model
  achieves $\text{mAP}_{50\text{-}95} = \mathbf{0.7651}$,
  $\text{mAP}_{50} = 0.962$, precision $= 0.960$, and recall
  $= 0.915$---the best result across all iterations.  All experiments
  are conducted on NVIDIA H100~PCIe GPUs at McMaster University's
  Grace HPC cluster.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ====================================================================

Detecting small, fast-moving rockets in surveillance footage poses unique
challenges for modern object detectors.  The targets of interest typically
occupy fewer than $30{\times}30$ pixels in $960{\times}960$ frames, can appear
at \emph{any} orientation ($0$--$360^{\circ}$), and are subject to imaging
degradation including motion blur, compression artefacts, and varying
illumination.

The YOLO family of detectors~\citep{ultralytics2025yolo} offers an attractive
balance between speed and accuracy for real-time deployment.  However, vanilla
YOLO architectures are optimised for objects at strides $\{8, 16, 32\}$, which
may miss very small targets.  The \textbf{P2 variant} introduces an additional
detection head at stride~$4$, quadrupling the spatial resolution of the finest
feature map.

This report documents the \emph{complete training journey} across three major
pipeline iterations:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{V1}: Architecture upgrade to \textsc{Yolo26s-P2}, COCO hard
        negative mining, and a three-stage fine-tuning protocol
        ($0.694 \to 0.750$).
  \item \textbf{V2}: Optimizer correction (SGD), extended Stage~1b training
        ($0.750 \to 0.762$), but persistent fine-tuning regression.
  \item \textbf{V3}: Regime-consistent training with gradient accumulation,
        native Albumentations integration, and aggressive small-target
        augmentation ($0.762 \to \mathbf{0.7651}$).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent Our contributions include:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Architecture upgrade}: adoption of \textsc{Yolo26s-P2} with a
        stride-4 detection head at $+22\%$ GFLOPs cost.
  \item \textbf{COCO hard negative mining}: 4{,}000 background images
        ($\sim\!25\%$ of training set) to suppress false positives.
  \item \textbf{Training regime consistency}: identification of the
        DDP-to-single-GPU regime shock as the root cause of fine-tuning
        regression, and resolution via gradient accumulation (\texttt{nbs=128}).
  \item \textbf{Native augmentation integration}: discovery and use of
        Ultralytics' \texttt{augmentations} parameter, replacing the
        non-functional monkey-patch approach under DDP.
  \item \textbf{Robustness-enhanced augmentation}: 9~custom Albumentations
        transforms targeting sensor noise, motion blur, compression, and
        illumination variation.
  \item \textbf{Failure analysis}: documentation of failed attempts across
        all three iterations and the lessons learned.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{COCO Hard Negative Mining}
\label{sec:coco_neg}

To suppress false positives, we inject \textbf{4{,}000 pure background images}
from MS~COCO~\citep{lin2014coco} into the training set.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
  \caption{COCO Hard Negative Mining Pipeline}
  \label{alg:coco}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require COCO train2017 + val2017 images (${\sim}123$K total)
    \Require Target count $N = 4{,}000$
    \State Download and extract COCO images (cached after first run)
    \State $S \gets \textsc{RandomSample}(\text{all COCO images}, N, \text{seed}=0)$
    \For{each image $s_i \in S$}
    \State Copy $s_i$ to \texttt{images/train/coco\_neg\_\{i\}.jpg}
    \State Create empty label file \texttt{labels/train/coco\_neg\_\{i\}.txt}
    \EndFor
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The empty label files ensure YOLO treats these images as containing \emph{zero}
objects, providing pure background supervision.

\section{Model Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
%% ====================================================================

\subsection{YOLO26s Baseline}

YOLO26~\citep{ultralytics2025yolo} is the latest generation of the Ultralytics
YOLO family, featuring an updated backbone and a dual-head design.  The
\textbf{small} variant (\textsc{Yolo26s}) has ${\sim}10$M parameters and
natively detects at strides $\{8, 16, 32\}$.

\subsection{P2 Variant: Stride-4 Detection Head}

The \textsc{Yolo26s-P2} variant adds a \textbf{P2 detection head} at
stride~$4$, which operates on a feature map with $4{\times}$ the spatial

%% ---- V1 ----
\subsection{V1: Initial Pipeline}
\label{sec:v1}

\subsubsection{Baseline (train63)}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Model}: \textsc{Yolo26s} (no P2 head), image size $[544, 960]$
  \item \textbf{Epochs}: 420, \textbf{Scheduler}: linear decay,
        $\text{lr}_0 = 0.01$, $\text{lrf} = 0.01$
  \item \textbf{Augmentation}: aggressive---mosaic$=1.0$, cutmix$=0.1$,
        scale$=0.3$, close\_mosaic$=80$
  \item \textbf{COCO negatives}: none
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Result}: epoch~419, mAP$_{50} = 0.948$,
mAP$_{50\text{-}95} = 0.694$.

\subsubsection{Stage~I: P2 Architecture + COCO Negatives (train76/77)}
\label{sec:v1_stage1}

Stage~I introduces two major changes: (1)~upgrade to \textsc{Yolo26s-P2} and
(2)~injection of 4{,}000 COCO hard negatives.  Augmentation is reduced
(mosaic $1.0 \to 0.8$, cutmix $0.1 \to 0.0$, scale $0.3 \to 0.15$).

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{V1 Stage~I configuration.}
  \label{tab:v1_stage1}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Parameter}        & \textbf{train76} & \textbf{train77} \\
    \midrule
    GPUs                      & 3 (H100)         & 4 (H100)         \\
    COCO negatives            & 1{,}000          & 4{,}000          \\
    Patience                  & 30               & 50               \\
    close\_mosaic             & 150              & 250              \\
    \midrule
    Best mAP$_{50\text{-}95}$ & 0.652 (ep335)    & 0.614 (ep267)    \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Critical issue.}  Both runs terminated via early stopping
\emph{before} the \texttt{close\_mosaic} epoch.  The model \textbf{never
  trained on full-resolution images}---it only saw $\frac{1}{4}$-scale mosaic
composites, severely limiting small-target localisation.

\subsubsection{Stage~II: Close-Mosaic Fine-Tuning (train78)}
\label{sec:v1_stage2}

Fine-tuning from \texttt{train77/best.pt} with mosaic \emph{completely
  disabled}: $\text{lr}_0 = 0.002$, cosine schedule, 100 epochs,
patience$= 40$.

\noindent\textbf{Result}: mAP$_{50\text{-}95}$ \textbf{jumped from $0.614$ to
  $0.720$} ($+17.3\%$)---the single largest improvement in V1.

\subsubsection{Stage~III: Ultra-Fine Polish (train81)}
\label{sec:v1_stage3}

Very low learning rate ($\text{lr}_0 = 0.0003$, SGD explicitly set), cosine
decay, 60 epochs.  Augmentation further reduced.

\noindent\textbf{Result}: mAP$_{50\text{-}95} = \mathbf{0.750}$ ($+4.2\%$),
mAP$_{50} = 0.958$, precision $= 0.961$, recall $= 0.915$.

\subsubsection{V1 Failed Attempts}

\paragraph{train79.}  Used \texttt{optimizer=``auto''} which selected AdamW and
overrode the specified $\text{lr}_0$.  Aborted after 1 epoch.

\paragraph{train80.}  Fixed optimizer to SGD but a configuration issue
prevented results from being saved.

\paragraph{Lesson.}  When fine-tuning, \textbf{always set the optimizer
  explicitly}.

%% ---- V2 ----
\subsection{V2: Optimizer Correction and Extended Training}
\label{sec:v2}

V2 addressed V1's optimizer inconsistency (auto/AdamW in early stages vs.\
explicit SGD in Stage~III) by using SGD throughout.

\subsubsection{Stage~1b: Extended DDP Training}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{GPUs}: 4$\times$H100 DDP, batch$=32$/GPU (effective 128)
  \item \textbf{Optimizer}: SGD, $\text{lr}_0 = 0.0005$, $\text{lrf} = 0.1$,
        cosine schedule
  \item \textbf{Epochs}: 300, patience$=0$ (run to completion)
  \item \textbf{Augmentation}: mosaic$=0.2$, close\_mosaic$=30$, mixup$=0.02$,
        scale$=0.08$
  \item \textbf{Custom Albumentations}: Attempted via monkey-patch on
        \texttt{Albumentations} class
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Result}: mAP$_{50\text{-}95} = \mathbf{0.7624}$ (epoch~283),
the best result in V2.

\subsubsection{V2 Stage~2/3: Fine-Tuning Regression}

Despite the improved Stage~1b baseline, fine-tuning stages \emph{consistently
  degraded} performance:

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{V2 fine-tuning regression.}
  \label{tab:v2_regression}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llcc@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Stage}  & \textbf{Configuration}                      & \textbf{mAP$_{50\text{-}95}$} & \textbf{$\Delta$} \\
    \midrule
    V2 Stage~1b     & 4-GPU DDP, batch=128, rect=False            & 0.7624                        & ---                \\
    V2 Stage~2      & 1-GPU, batch=32, rect=True, SGD lr=0.0003  & 0.7550                        & $-0.0074$          \\
    V2 Stage~3      & 1-GPU, batch=32, rect=True, SGD lr=0.0001  & $<0.755$                      & $<-0.007$          \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Root cause analysis.}  Three compounding factors caused the
regression (detailed in Section~\ref{sec:regime_analysis}):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Regime shock}: 4-GPU DDP $\to$ single-GPU changed effective
        batch size, BatchNorm statistics, and data ordering.
  \item \textbf{Rectangular inference}: \texttt{rect=True} in fine-tuning
        vs.\ \texttt{rect=False} in Stage~1b altered input distributions.
  \item \textbf{Non-functional augmentations}: The monkey-patched
        Albumentations were \emph{never applied} under DDP
        (Section~\ref{sec:augmentation_discovery}).
\end{enumerate}

%% ---- V3 ----
\subsection{V3: Regime-Consistent Robustness-Enhanced Pipeline}
\label{sec:v3}

V3 was designed from the ground up to address all three root causes of V2's
fine-tuning regression.

\subsubsection{Design Principles}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Regime consistency}: Single GPU with gradient accumulation
        (\texttt{nbs=128}, accumulate$=4$) throughout both phases, maintaining
        the same effective batch size as 4-GPU DDP while eliminating regime
        transitions.
  \item \textbf{Native augmentation}: Use Ultralytics' built-in
        \texttt{augmentations} parameter instead of monkey-patching.
  \item \textbf{Aggressive small-target augmentation}: \texttt{scale} increased
        $3{\times}$ ($0.08 \to 0.25$), \texttt{erasing} enabled ($0.0 \to 0.30$),
        and 9~custom Albumentations transforms.
  \item \textbf{Progressive augmentation reduction}: Strong augmentation in
        Phase~1 for robust feature learning, gentle augmentation in Phase~2
        for precision refinement.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Phase~1: Small-Target + Robustness Joint Training (250 epochs)}
\label{sec:v3_phase1}

Phase~1 continues from V2 Stage~1b's best weights
(\texttt{stage1b/best.pt}, mAP$_{50\text{-}95} = 0.762$).

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \caption{V3 Phase~1 configuration.}
  \label{tab:v3_phase1}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
    \toprule

    \midrule
    GPU                   & 1$\times$H100 PCIe (80\,GB)                          \\

    Image size            & $960{\times}960$                                     \\

    Optimizer             & SGD, $\text{lr}_0=0.0003$, $\text{lrf}=0.1$, cosine  \\

    \midrule
    mosaic / close\_mosaic & 0.15 / 40                                           \\

\subsubsection{Phase~2: Low-LR Fine-Tuning (80 epochs)}
\label{sec:v3_phase2}

Phase~2 inherits Phase~1's best weights and applies reduced augmentation
with a lower learning rate.  Critically, \texttt{rect=False} and
\texttt{nbs=128} are maintained---the exact parameters that V2 changed,
causing regression.

\subsection{The Augmentation Integration Discovery}
\label{sec:augmentation_discovery}

A critical finding during V3 development was that the monkey-patch approach
used in V1/V2 for injecting custom Albumentations \textbf{does not work under
  DDP}.  Ultralytics' DDP implementation uses
\texttt{generate\_ddp\_file()}~\citep{ultralytics2025yolo} to create a
temporary training script for each subprocess.  The monkey-patch, applied in
the main process, is not propagated to these temporary files.

Investigation of Ultralytics' source code revealed a \emph{native}
\texttt{augmentations} parameter:

\begin{verbatim}
# ultralytics/data/augment.py (v8_transforms):
augmentations = getattr(hyp, "augmentations", None)
if augmentations:
    transforms.append(
        Albumentations(augmentations=augmentations)
    )
\end{verbatim}

By passing transforms via \texttt{model.train(augmentations=...)}, the
augmentations are serialised into the hyperparameter object and correctly
propagated to all processes.  This was the \textbf{first time} custom
Albumentations were reliably applied in this project's multi-GPU training
history.

\subsection{Training Regime Consistency}
\label{sec:regime_analysis}

The most important finding across all three iterations is that
\textbf{fine-tuning performance is highly sensitive to training regime

\subsection{Gradient Accumulation Equivalence}

V3's use of \texttt{nbs=128} with \texttt{batch=32} on a single GPU creates
an accumulation factor of $128/32 = 4$.  This is mathematically equivalent to
4-GPU DDP with batch$=32$/GPU in terms of:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Gradient computation}: $\nabla L = \frac{1}{128}\sum_{i=1}^{128} \nabla \ell_i$ in both cases.
  \item \textbf{Learning rate scaling}: The effective learning rate per sample
        is identical.
  \item \textbf{Weight updates}: Updates occur every 128 samples in both
        configurations.
\end{itemize}

\noindent The key difference is that single-GPU accumulation avoids DDP's
communication overhead and, critically, avoids the
\texttt{generate\_ddp\_file} mechanism that broke custom augmentations.

\subsection{Why V1 Stage~I Degraded Performance}

The mAP$_{50\text{-}95}$ drop from $0.694$ (baseline) to $0.614$ (Stage~I) is
attributable to three compounding factors:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{COCO negative dilution}: 4{,}000 background images (25\%)
        force a harder decision boundary.
  \item \textbf{Mosaic starvation}: Mosaic composites reduce each image to
        $\frac{1}{4}$ resolution; early stopping prevented the
        \texttt{close\_mosaic} phase from ever executing.
  \item \textbf{Architectural cold start}: The P2 head's new layers require
        extended training to specialise.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Close-Mosaic Timing Problem}

The interaction between \texttt{close\_mosaic} and \texttt{patience} is a
subtle failure mode:
\[
  \text{mosaic\_off\_epoch} = \text{total\_epochs} - \text{close\_mosaic}
  = 600 - 250 = 350
\]
But early stopping triggered at epoch~317 ($<350$), so the transition never
occurred.  V1 Stage~II solved this by performing close-mosaic fine-tuning as a
separate stage.  V3 Phase~1 avoids the issue entirely by setting
\texttt{patience=0} (no early stopping) with a moderate
\texttt{close\_mosaic=40}.

\subsection{Progressive Augmentation Reduction}

The augmentation strategy follows a curriculum learning
principle~\citep{bengio2009curriculum}:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Phase~1 (exploration)}: Heavy augmentation (mosaic, mixup,
        scale$=0.25$, erasing$=0.30$, 9~Albumentations at high probability)
        forces the model to learn robust, invariant features.
  \item \textbf{Phase~2 (exploitation)}: Gentle augmentation (no mosaic/mixup,
        reduced scale/erasing, 8~Albumentations at ${\sim}60\%$ probability)
        allows the model to refine decision boundaries on cleaner data.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Effect of COCO Negative Count}

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Impact of COCO negative sample count on initial training.}

More negatives produce a harder training problem but improve deployment

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% ====================================================================

We have presented the complete training evolution of a small-rocket detection
system across three pipeline iterations, progressing from a vanilla
\textsc{Yolo26s} baseline (mAP$_{50\text{-}95} = 0.694$) to a final
\textsc{Yolo26s-P2} model achieving
mAP$_{50\text{-}95} = \mathbf{0.7651}$ ($+10.2\%$) and
mAP$_{50} = 0.962$.  The key insights are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Training regime consistency is paramount.}  The transition from
        4-GPU DDP to single-GPU fine-tuning caused persistent regression in V1
        and V2.  V3's gradient accumulation (\texttt{nbs=128}) maintains
        regime consistency while avoiding DDP complexity.

  \item \textbf{Augmentation integration must be verified.}  Monkey-patched
        Albumentations silently failed under DDP for the entire V1/V2 training
        history.  The native \texttt{augmentations} parameter provides reliable
        integration.

  \item \textbf{Small-target augmentation matters.}  Increasing \texttt{scale}
        from 0.08 to 0.25 and enabling \texttt{erasing=0.30} directly improved
        the model's ability to detect targets at the COCO-small scale.

  \item \textbf{Progressive augmentation reduction} follows curriculum learning
        principles: heavy augmentation for feature diversity, then minimal
        augmentation for precision refinement.

  \item \textbf{Close-mosaic must be decoupled from early stopping.}  Either
        disable patience during the close-mosaic window, or implement it as a
        separate training phase.

  \item \textbf{Deployment readiness.}  The final model (9.66M parameters,
        26.4~GFLOPs) runs at $960{\times}960$ resolution, suitable for
        real-time inference on GPU-equipped edge devices.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent A detailed V3 training pipeline specification, including all
hyperparameters, augmentation configurations, and reproduction instructions,
is provided in the companion document
\texttt{V3\_Training\_Pipeline.md}.
